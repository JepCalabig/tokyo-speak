Write a clear Japanese lesson on Japanese particle nuances. Write the explanations in natural Japanese. 
Keep the teaching style practical and image-based, not textbook-stiff.

Cover these particles and contrasts:
wa vs ga
o
ni
de
to
mo
kara / made
no
extra distinctions: ga vs o with potential verbs, ni vs to for "with," particle + wa for contrast, ga meaning "but"
end with a short decision guide

Teaching points, apply them to every particle:
Say what the particle is pointing at in the sentence.
Say whether it marks old information, new information, contrast, or a relationship between two elements.
Give one simple image or hook for the particle.
Show a default everyday use.
Show a use that changes the nuance or makes the sentence sound more specific.
Contrast it with the particle learners most often confuse it with.
Keep notes short: job, feel, common mix-up.

For every example:
1. give the Japanese sentence in kana/kanji
2. give romaji
3. give an English translation

Use headings, short sections, and compact nuance notes after each particle. After the decision guide, offer to drill one pair next: wa/ga, ni/de, or ni/e.

Audience: a learner who already knows basic Japanese and wants nuance, not a first-day intro.

Include these sample contrasts somewhere in the lesson, using the same example format:
densha ga kimashita
kono densha ga ueno ni ikimasu ka
kono densha wa ueno ni ikimasu ka
